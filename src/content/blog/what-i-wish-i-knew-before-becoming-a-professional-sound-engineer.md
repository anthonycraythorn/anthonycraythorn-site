I have been working in the entertainment industry as a sound engineer for over a decade now and learnt many lessons a long the way. I did not manage the career I have had without a lot of hard work, trial & error and a bit of luck on the side. Here are 5 of the most important things I wish I knew before starting my journey as a Sound Engineer. 

## Brutal Hours

In my early career I distinctly remember doing several festivals where the days turned into nights which turned into load outs which turned into driving long distances home. Live sound engineering often involves being one of the first suppliers to show up and the last to leave an event. Be prepared for long days. No employer is actively calling you up and saying “Hey are you keen for an 18hr shift today?” - these hours just end up happening. Usually from poor scheduling or crewing. It happens, get used to it. 

Unfortunately, even as you progress in your career these long hours still manage to bleed into your life. I once worked a 24 hours shift pulling a show out of the Sydney Opera House overnight after working all day mixing the show. I remember getting back to my hotel around 11am in the morning, grabbing my suitcase and heading straight for the airport looking dishevelled and probably not smelling the best…  

## Chasing the fun gigs is a trap

Most new engineers want to work on the big festivals, the large PA’s or with the most renowned artists. Its a natural because most engineers probably got interested in the job after going to see a show of this scale. However, walk into a warehouse early in your career and start pestering your boss to be crewed for that gig will be a sure fire way to getting thrown on the gig with a lectern mic and a powerpoint. 

Your progression is generally earned on your merit. Expect to put in a lot of hard work doing the difficult jobs that no one really wants to touch. Prove your work ethic early on and don’t be afraid to volunteer yourself for the hardest tasks. When you consistently show up, work hard and do a good job you will find yourself naturally being asked to do bigger and better work. 

## No one knows what *Good Sound* actually is

Most people, and I mean everyone from punters to artists don’t know what or how to describe a ‘good sounding’ show. You might get the odd compliment here and there, maybe wondering if that person is truly genuine or just saying it because the sound console is right next to the bar which is a good spot to lean your drink and enjoy the show. 

More often than not, a great sounding show is one where the mix and sound system did not get in the way of the artists performance. When there is excellent sound, rarely will anyone even notice the mix or give a second thought on how intelligible the vocals were. Most likely, a punter will attribute their auditary enjoyment of the performance directly to the artist/band themselves. 

When I hear comments like “their voice is so present and powerful” I also attribute that to the work of the sound engineer in delivering that emotion to the audience. 

The bottom line is: don’t expect praise for your mix, even when you think it was the best sounding show you have ever worked on.

## Financially Lucrative at a Cost

I have earned some really good money at times in my career. It has allowed me to purchase a home in Australia with my partner and also travel and go on holidays. 

However it is not without a very real cost. To be earning higher amounts in this industry you generally have to be doing the more demanding work that requires you to be on the road and away from home. 

When you end up on a tour typically your accomodation will be paid for and you will receiver per diems (meals allowance) to pay for your food and drink while you are away from home. This is an excellent way to save a lot of money very quickly if you are able to be conservative and live off these per diems. Every cent of your income can then be put into savings. 

This is how I saved for a house deposit after taking a job that had me touring for over 12 months straight. 

However, the real cost to this is being away from home for so long. This can put real strain on relationships and families. It makes starting a family practically impossible. Its also common for people to loose a sense of ‘home’. Friendships and relationships breakdown or become scarce. 

You end up spending all your time with your work colleagues on days off because there is nothing else to do. Your sense of routine can be shattered.

Many people struggle with this and it can have a lasting impact on ones mental health. 

I’m not saying not to tour, in fact I think everyone should! Its a great way to save money fast and is incredibly fun. But be sure to have a reality check every now and then. Go home and ground yourself occasionally.

## People Skills

Majority of the job is people skills. This is one hill I will absolutely die on. 

I have never worked on a show where I was not required to communicate critical information  with an artist/producer/client/stakeholder/employer/colleague. And the way you communicate information with others is paramount to the success of your career. 

If you can have a friendly conversation with an artist, make them feel comfortable & supported on stage, they will not only perform better, they will want to hire you again. See the difference between never being called again and your phone ringing constantly is how well you got along with everyone at the last gig. 

The cliche grumpy sound engineer is real and the truth is no one wants to work with them. Don’t be the grumpy sound engineer. 

Check in with your artists, ask them if they need anything. Speak to the producer/client and ensure they are happy with everything. Offer solutions not excuses. Give them as many reasons as possible to rave about you and the service you offered. 

*This skill is equal if not more important than your technical abilities as an engineer*

You can progressively become a better engineer over time. Your ear will get better at listening and you will learn more techniques. People will forgive a basic mix/setup if you are a junior engineer as long as you get the basics right. People will not forgive a bad attitude. 

One negative experience from an artists/producer/client can put a serious roadblock in your career. So always be kind, communicate effectively and check in with the right people regularly.
